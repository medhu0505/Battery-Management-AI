# Methodology

This page covers how each part was modelled, trained and evaluated, and why. Every number quoted here is regenerated by `python -m bms_copilot.train` and stored in `artifacts/model_report.json`.

**The guiding rule: every model is scored against ground truth it never saw, and next to a baseline that exists today.**

---

## 1. A simulator worth trusting

A single laptop gives one battery's history and no ground truth. Training and scoring battery AI needs thousands of battery-years, known internal states and injected faults, so the first step was a physics-based fleet simulator.

### Cell model

- **Electrode-level open-circuit voltage** (`physics/electrode.py`). The cell voltage is the cathode potential minus the anode potential, each evaluated at its own lithium content. The open-circuit potential curves for NMC811 and graphite are the Chen et al. (2020) fits used in PyBaMM.
- **Three degradation modes** shift how the two electrodes line up:
  - **LLI**, loss of lithium inventory (SEI growth, plating);
  - **LAM<sub>pe</sub>**, loss of cathode active material;
  - **LAM<sub>ne</sub>**, loss of anode active material.

  LAM is modelled as loss of *lithiated* material, so it also consumes lithium. Each mode distorts the dQ/dV curve in its own way, which is what makes them identifiable from charging data.
- **Dynamics** (`physics/ecm.py`): a second-order (2RC) equivalent circuit with charge/discharge hysteresis, temperature-dependent resistance and a lumped thermal node. The digital twin deliberately uses a *simpler* 1RC model, so that its learned residual has real unmodelled dynamics to capture.
- **Pack**: 3 cells in series (3S1P), 4.5 Ah NMC811/graphite cells, 4.2 V max, which is representative of a 15" laptop.

### Ageing model

`physics/aging.py` turns each day's usage into degradation through semi-empirical laws, each tied to one mode:

| Mechanism | Mode | Driven by |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar SEI growth | LLI | √time, Arrhenius in temperature, and a state-of-charge stress factor S(SoC) = 0.3 + 1.2·SoC² + 2.5·(time above 95 %) |
| Cycling SEI and cracking | LLI | Throughput (equivalent full cycles), worse at high C-rate and depth of discharge |
| Lithium plating | LLI | Cold temperature combined with fast charging |
| Cathode degradation | LAM<sub>pe</sub> | Time at high voltage |
| Anode degradation | LAM<sub>ne</sub> | Charge rate and depth of discharge |

Rate constants were tuned so simulated laptops lose capacity at realistic rates: on average about 2 % per 100 days under legacy charging, reaching 80 % health after a few years of typical use.

### Fleet

`sim/fleet.py` ages every device day by day, all devices × 3 cells in lockstep (fully vectorised), and records a telemetry snapshot every 14 days.

- **Five usage profiles**: hybrid office worker, always docked, road warrior, creator/gamer and student, each with its own throughput, load, charging habits and unplug time.
- **Four climates** (temperate, warm, hot, cold), representing indoor ambient conditions.
- **Manufacturing variation**: cell-to-cell capacity and electrode-balance spread, and a per-device current-sensor gain error (σ = 0.6 %).
- **Sensor realism** (`sim/sensors.py`): 1 mV voltage noise, 1 mV ADC quantisation and the gain error. The models see what a real battery front-end would measure, never the true state.
- **Injected defects**:
  - one supplier lot (L2317, 12 % of devices) where 40 % of packs develop accelerated anode loss 30–300 days into service, with a 1.2 % background rate elsewhere;
  - swelling cells and internal micro-shorts at random onset times.
- **Two cohorts**:
  - **history**: 200 devices simulated to end of life, used for training;
  - **live**: 240 devices at mixed ages, whose futures are also simulated but used *only* for scoring.

## 2. Health sensing on the device: incremental capacity analysis

During an opportunistic slow constant-current charge (for example the overnight part of an adaptive plan), `edge/ica.py`:

1. smooths raw voltage samples with a Savitzky–Golay filter (window 21, order 3);
2. enforces monotonic voltage;
3. integrates charge into 2 mV voltage bins, which yields dQ/dV directly, with no numerical differentiation of noisy data;
4. smooths the dQ/dV curve;
5. extracts **peak height, peak position and area** in three voltage bands (3.55–3.75, 3.75–4.02 and 4.02–4.15 V), giving nine features.

The bands are part of the calibration package pushed over the air, so they can follow peaks as they drift with age. The extractor uses only NumPy, fixed-size buffers and no root finding, to fit a microcontroller budget.

## 3. Health models in the cloud: Gaussian processes

`cloud/gpr.py` is an exact Gaussian process implemented from scratch. It uses an ARD squared-exponential kernel plus white noise, with hyperparameters fitted by L-BFGS-B on the log marginal likelihood (analytic gradients, several restarts).

A GP was chosen over a neural network for three reasons: the training set is small (hundreds of devices), every estimate needs a **calibrated uncertainty**, and the learned length scales show which features matter.

| Model | Inputs | Output |
|---|---|---|
| SoH | 9 ICA features, internal resistance (DCIR), capture temperature | State of health ± σ |
| Degradation modes | The same routine features | LLI, LAM<sub>pe</sub>, LAM<sub>ne</sub> of the weakest cell |
| RUL | Current SoH, recent and lifetime fade rate, resistance, throughput, temperature, time at high SoC, charge rate, age | Days to 80 % SoH, trained on log(days + 30) so the interval is asymmetric and never negative |

**Evaluation**: the models are scored on held-out *devices*, not held-out snapshots, so the model has never seen any part of the battery it is predicting. The reported metrics are mean absolute error and the coverage of the 95 % interval; a calibrated model should reach about 95 %.

**Degradation-mode analysis** (`cloud/dma.py`) is a second, more precise path. When triage needs certainty, the device runs a full C/20 diagnostic capture and the three modes plus an ohmic offset are fitted by least squares to the measured charge curve. The fit stops early once the residual drops below 3 mAh.

## 4. Hybrid digital twin

`cloud/twin.py` combines:

- a **physics core**: a 1RC equivalent circuit re-parameterised per device (capacity from the SoH GP, the OCV table from the degradation modes, R0 from DCIR telemetry);
- a **learned residual**: ridge regression on engineered features that predicts the gap between the physics model and measured voltage (hysteresis, the missing second RC branch, temperature effects);
- a **what-if engine**: the ageing model projected forward under alternative charging policies for this device's actual usage.

**SoC estimation** (`edge/soc.py`) compares coulomb counting, an EKF with beginning-of-life parameters, and an EKF recalibrated from the twin, on an aged cell.

## 5. Early-warning detector

`edge/anomaly.py` watches five signals the battery front-end already produces:

- the rest-voltage spread between cells;
- self-discharge;
- measured minus twin-predicted temperature rise;
- pack strain above the healthy baseline;
- the growth rate of internal resistance.

1. **Healthy baseline.** Fit on defect-free fleet snapshots, after removing each signal's slow linear ageing trend. This detrending step is what took false alarms from 21 % to 0.2 % (see [lessons learned](lessons-learned.md)).
2. **Mahalanobis distance** against that baseline, with χ² thresholds at 0.99 (*watch*) and 0.9999 (*warning*).
3. **One-sided CUSUM** on the standardised signals that should not drift (self-discharge, thermal residual, strain), to catch slow changes before the distance crosses its threshold.
4. **Hard physical rules**, which raise *critical* independently of the statistics.
5. **Mechanism attribution.** The signal contributing most names the likely mechanism. Strain, self-discharge and thermal-residual alerts are treated as **safety precursors**. Cell divergence alone usually means imbalance or ageing, so it is routed to diagnostics rather than escalated as a safety issue.

**Evaluation**: false-warning rate on 8,818 healthy snapshots; for defective devices, the delay from true onset to the first warning and the lead time before the condition turns critical.

## 6. Adaptive charging

### Context model

`charging/context.py` learns each user's typical first unplug time (weekday and weekend) and daily energy need from their history. Calendar events override it: a flight means 100 % shortly before departure, and a light day means holding a lower charge.

### MILP optimiser

`charging/milp.py` plans each 15-minute step until the predicted unplug time.

- **Decision variables**:
  - charge rate split into a gentle segment (≤ 0.5 C) and a costlier fast segment;
  - a binary charger on/off variable (minimum current when on);
  - state of charge;
  - dwell above 80 % and 95 %;
  - a soft unmet-energy variable and a reserve-floor shortfall, which hedges against an early unplug.
- **Objective** (in €): linearised capacity damage + time-of-use electricity + a carbon shadow price + a heavy unmet-demand penalty. The piecewise-linear damage coefficients are **fitted from the ageing model** for this device's current state and forecast temperature. The optimiser therefore minimises the same physics the evaluation uses, rather than a hand-tuned proxy.
- **Constraints**:
  - SoC dynamics;
  - JEITA temperature derating;
  - a **thermal ceiling**, the current at which steady-state self-heating would breach the charge-temperature limit;
  - linear chord approximations of the constant-voltage taper near full charge.
- **Verify and re-plan.** Each plan is rolled out on the safety-gated twin. If the linear CV approximation was optimistic and the plan falls short, the shortfall is added to the internal target and the problem is re-solved, or a gentle top-up fills the idle steps before unplug. The best verified plan is returned.

Solved with HiGHS via `scipy.optimize.milp`.

### Reinforcement learning

`charging/rl.py` frames charging as an MDP:

- **State**: SoC, hours to unplug, ambient temperature, target, SoH band and a first-hour flag; 4,320 discrete states.
- **Actions**: five charge rates from 0 to 1.2 C.
- **Reward**: −(damage + energy cost), with penalties for missing the reserve floor or the target.

Tabular Q-learning runs on a vectorised environment, many episodes in parallel, about 900,000 per build, through the same safety-gated physics. Duplicate state-action updates within a batch are averaged.

**Evaluation**: 150 sessions sampled across profiles and scenarios. Legacy, MILP and RL charging are each simulated end to end on identical physics, comparing capacity loss, the share of targets met, energy cost and peak temperature.

## 7. TinyML: getting the policy onto the device

`edge/tinyml.py` compresses the RL policy into something a laptop's embedded controller or NPU could run:

1. **Knowledge distillation**: a small MLP learns the Q-table's action preferences from soft targets.
2. **DAgger**: the student is rolled out and the teacher labels the states the *student* visits, closing the train/deploy distribution gap.
3. **Structured pruning**: remove the 50 % of hidden neurons with the smallest weight norm, then fine-tune. Whole neurons are removed, so the shapes stay dense and NPU-friendly.
4. **Quantisation-aware training**: fine-tune with fake-quantised INT8 weights (per output channel) and activations (per tensor), using a straight-through estimator.
5. **ONNX export** (FP32 and INT8 QDQ), checked for decision parity with onnxruntime.
6. **Deadline guard**: a deterministic rule that forces just enough current to meet the target in the time left. It is cheap, verifiable and catches the rare cases where a compressed policy is too timid.

**Evaluation**: action agreement with the teacher is reported at every stage, but the deciding metric is the **closed-loop** share of targets met when the student actually controls the charger.

## 8. Agents, passport and grading

- **Agents** (`agents/workforce.py`): rule-based reasoning over model outputs, not LLM calls, so every decision is deterministic and auditable.
  - Triage calls the degradation-mode fit and compares each device's anode loss with the fleet expectation for its age and usage (robust z-scores).
  - Design feedback tests each supplier lot for excess anode loss with Mann–Whitney and binomial tests, Bonferroni-corrected.
  - It also estimates the adaptive-vs-legacy fade difference, matched by usage profile.
- **Passport** (`cloud/passport.py`): the fields follow the Annex XIII categories of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, with three access tiers and SHA-256 hash-chained versions. Values only a manufacturer can declare (carbon footprint, due diligence, recycled content) are explicit placeholders.
- **Second-life grading** (`cloud/grading.py`): A, B, C or F, using the *lower* bound of the RUL interval rather than the median. Hard safety gates (precursors, abnormal self-discharge) override any score.

## 9. The real laptop

`live/` applies the same ideas to the computer the server runs on:

1. **Data**:
   - `powercfg /batteryreport /xml` gives design capacity, up to a year of full-charge-capacity history, cycle count and usage;
   - WMI `root\wmi` gives live charge, voltage, power and AC state.
   - Entries that are physically impossible, claiming more active or standby time than the period they cover, are flagged and excluded.
2. **Forecast**: a GP with a smooth RBF component, a linear trend and noise, fitted to capacity over time. 4,000 posterior trajectories are sampled to get the distribution of the date the battery crosses 80, 70 and 60 % of design.
3. **Usage profile**:
   - share of time on AC;
   - time-weighted state of charge;
   - typical first-unplug hour;
   - daily energy drawn from the battery;
   - detection of a firmware charge limit, such as a 60 % or 80 % "battery care" mode.
4. **Calibrated what-if**: the fleet ageing model is scaled by a single rate factor *k*, fitted so that it reproduces this battery's observed fade. It then projects current habits, AI adaptive charging and always-100 %.
5. **Tonight's plan**: the MILP from section 6, starting from the live state of charge and the learned unplug time, honouring any detected charge limit unless a trip is coming.

The real-laptop path **observes but does not actuate**. Charge current is controlled by the manufacturer's embedded-controller firmware, so the plan is advisory.
